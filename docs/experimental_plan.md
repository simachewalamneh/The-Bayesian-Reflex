## Protocol rules
- **A stage validates only the component it tests.** "The Bayesian Reflex" is evaluated only at E7 (closed loop, with ablations).
- Fix pass/fail criteria before coding; >=10 seeds; report mean +/- sd; every positive test has a negative control that must fail.
- E1-E3 are validation tests of the implementation; E4-E8 carry the research claims.

## Decisions recorded
- E1: coverage tolerance = 3 binomial SE; ranks from posterior samples (reusable for non-Gaussian methods).
- E3: z uses the one-step-ahead predictive, var = sigma_{t-1}^2 + sigma_y^2, computed before the update.
- E5: tracking vs retention controlled by one forgetting factor lambda (VCL, paper 9.2).
- E6-A uses argmax of variance; for a GP with constant noise, A and B coincide for single queries.

- E3: independent z_t under the correct model; negative controls = wrong sigma_y and stale (post-update) variance; unknown-variance variant gives Student-t(2a_{t-1}).
- E4: hyperparameters fixed and shared (sf=1, ell=1, sigma_y=0.2); the PC-FSVI used is a minimal stand-in (inducing-point q(u), PC settling), whose fixed point is the conjugate update, so near-exact agreement with the GP is expected by construction. M<=5 inducing points breaks it (control).
- E4 latency: PC-FSVI is O(M^2), constant in t; exact rank-one GP is O(t^2), so the advantage over the exact GP appears only at large t (crossover ~ a few hundred steps in our run); vs batch refit O(t^3) it is immediate.
- E4-real: your code via adapter, same criteria. Streaming one point at a time is ~10x less accurate than 50-point chunks (1e-4 vs 7e-6 RMSE) because jitter accumulates per chained update. Latency 5.7 ms/update (constant) is dominated by 150 iterations that each re-invert M x M matrices; with lr=1.0 the update is exact Newton, and n_iters=1 gave identical accuracy at 0.10 ms in a side check (Gaussian likelihood only).
- E5-A: forgetting factor lambda and process noise are the same filter (var_pred = var/lambda = var + sigma_eta^2 when lambda_t = var/(var+sigma_eta^2)); a constant lambda* reproduces the Kalman variance only asymptotically (the pre-registered t>=100 tolerance was too tight; diff is 7e-9 by t>=150, a post-hoc diagnostic). Controls: static belief and mismatched sigma_eta (x0.2, x5) lose calibration. An EMA tuned to the steady-state Kalman gain ties the Kalman filter in RMSE and recovery; the Kalman advantage is calibrated uncertainty, the transient, and a principled gain. Untuned EMAs (alpha 0.02 / 0.5) are worse (RMSE 0.259 / 0.290 vs 0.154). The 1.96-sigma recovery metric can be gamed by inflated uncertainty (sigma_eta x5 'recovers' in 2 steps), so also report the fixed-band version.
- E5-A miss diagnosis (deterministic, longdouble): lambda* = P/(P+q) = 1-K_ss exactly; both variance maps share the fixed point P_ss; Kalman contracts at (1-K)^2=0.819 but constant-lambda at lambda*=0.905, so |diff| ~ lambda*^t (measured log-slope -0.0999 vs ln lambda* -0.1000); diff=1.0583e-6 at t=100, <1e-6 from t=101, 7.2e-9 at t=150. Ruled out: P_t vs P_{t|t-1}, lambda definition, float64 error (2.8e-17). Proposed v2 criteria (to be added beside, not replacing, the original): both converge to analytic P_ss (|Var(T=1000)-P_ss|<1e-12) and |diff| decays at rate lambda* (log-slope within 2% of ln lambda*).
- E5-B findings (stand-in, 5 seeds x 100 trials for B2/B3): kernel-correlated process noise gives a calibrated exact oracle (cov95 0.948-0.951, innovations N(0,1)); the stand-in matches it to 9e-6 (near-exact by construction). Tracking vs retention is real: raising sigma_eta (0 -> 0.02 -> 0.05 -> 0.1 -> 0.2) improves B2 recovery (inf -> 60 -> 23 -> 12 -> 8 steps) while the retention RMSE in the unobserved region grows (ratio 1.01 -> 1.37 -> 1.60 -> 1.54 -> 1.63); tracking error itself is best near sigma_eta 0.02-0.05. Multiplicative forgetting (S /= lambda) is UNSAFE for unobserved regions: lambda=0.9 recovers in 31 steps but retention RMSE explodes (0.25 -> 10.5) because variance grows like lambda^-t where no data arrives; forget toward the prior instead (additive noise, or Lambda <- lambda*Lambda + (1-lambda)*K^-1). Mapping to the real code: pc_infer's beta scales the prior precision (= lambda), and a drift time update would pass prior_cov = S + sigma_eta^2 K_zz.
- E5-B-real: same-data comparison (20 trials). Real == stand-in == oracle for sigma_eta in {0,0.05,0.1} to <=6e-5 RMSE and identical recovery steps; the B3 retention miss therefore reproduces with the real engine (ratio 1.60) and is NOT an engine/basis limit. beta (prior-precision scaling) behaves as lambda: beta=0.95 safe (retention ratio 1.81), beta=0.9 unstable (24.8 real vs 25.1 stand-in; 42 with 500 trials, so the value is trial-dependent in the unstable regime). The only real-vs-stand-in miss (beta=0.9 absolute RMSE diff 0.067 > 0.01) is 1.2% relative; an absolute tolerance is the wrong tool in a regime where variance grows like beta^-t. n_iters=5 equals 150 to 5e-13.
- E6 findings (exact GP oracle, 20 paired seeds, T=200): variance sampling cuts MSE at T=30 by 41% (0.0083 vs 0.0140, p=1.6e-3) and reaches MSE<0.01 in 25 vs 34 queries (1.36x) and <0.02 in 14 vs 20 (1.43x), but at T=100 the advantage is gone (0.00275 vs 0.00286, p=0.77) and MSE<0.005 takes 56 vs 60 (1.07x): once the domain is covered, the remaining error is observation noise. It is boundary-seeking (33% of queries at |x|>2.5 vs 17% random). For a GP with fixed hyperparameters the variance policy ignores y, i.e. it is a sequential design; information gain picks identical points (single queries). Thompson sampling (maximise f) cuts cumulative regret(100) by 92 (last-25-step regret 1.3% of random's); anti-policies are worse (controls pass). Under functional drift the variance policy lowers tracking RMSE by 6.6% (p<0.01). A5 (stand-in/real vs exact GP MSE within 5% under each engine's own picks) missed (14-40%): sequences diverge after 2-3 steps from near-ties (identical picks 38-57%), but with the SAME queries and noise the engines agree to 4e-5 (stand-in) and 8e-5 (real). Post-hoc diagnostic; the original criterion stays recorded.
- E6 A1 diagnosis (200 paired seeds, exact GP, T=100; post-hoc, the 20-seed numbers above stay recorded): variance sampling lowers MSE by 44% at T=10, 36% at T=20, 21% at T=30 (p=6.6e-5), 14% at T=45 (p=0.009), then 0.5% at T=60 (p=0.92) and 4.1% at T=100 (95% CI -13%..+5%, p=0.38; Wilcoxon p=0.31). The 20-seed estimate at T=30 (-41%) was an overestimate (200 seeds: -21%). Queries to mean MSE<0.02/<0.01/<0.005: 13/27/63 vs 20/35/64 (ratios 1.54/1.30/1.02). So the active policy accelerates the early phase only; with the noise floor reached, random sampling catches up. A1 stays recorded as a miss; it is a genuine null at T=100, not noise in the criterion.
- E6 v2 out-of-sample confirmation (fresh seeds 5000-5199, T=200, exact GP): queries to mean MSE<0.02 / <0.01 / <0.005: variance 14/29/61 vs random 19/37/67 (ratios 1.36 [CI 1.29-1.58], 1.28 [1.12-1.44], 1.10 [1.00-1.23, info]); T=30 MSE -19.9% (p=1.9e-5); non-inferiority at T=100 and T=200: relative difference -3.9% / -3.6%, upper 95% bound +5.6% / +5.5% (< +10% margin). The v2 criteria and margins were fixed after the diagnosis and before this run; the original A1 (T=100) stays recorded as a miss. What E6 supports: uncertainty-driven querying speeds up the early phase, does not hurt later, helps under drift (-6.6%), and Thompson sampling cuts regret. What it does not test: the closed loop with prediction-error feedback (E7); for a fixed-hyperparameter GP the variance policy ignores y (a sequential design).
- E7 exact-grid results (frozen thresholds): FULL mean RMSE 0.184 vs 0.212 without action (-13%, p=7e-13), 0.502 without drift model, 0.196 without error feedback, 0.515 with none of the three (-64%). Median recovery after the shift: FULL 10 steps vs 17 without error feedback; pre-shift harm of error feedback upper bound +0.7% (limit +5%); FULL pre-shift cov95 0.958 (slightly conservative, limit +-0.03); A3 mean z^2 is 1.00 pre-shift and 14.1 in the 5 steps after the shift; wrong-sigma_y control z^2 = 1.84 (must fail calibration, does). Caveats: recovery speed alone does not certify correctness (the wrong-sigma_y control recovers faster, 7 steps, with only +8% RMSE), so C3 must be read with C4/C5; the plasticity rule (window 5, clip 1..100) is a hand-designed heuristic, validated in one environment with one shift; not yet run on the stand-in/real engines; the paper's LUT/ellipsoidal machinery is not involved (E4b/E8 deferred).
